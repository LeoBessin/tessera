Setting Board

Trial work, laid before anything is committed to the wall. Every form below is generated by code from one seed — no models, no textures — so what you are choosing between is generators, not pictures. Change the seed to see whether a direction holds across more than the one roll that happened to look good.

01 — the decision

Choose the mood

One panel, mid-restoration, under six treatments. The cold half is mosaic still broken; colour returns from the right as it is re-laid, which is the progress bar from ADR 6. Judge the whole frame, not the swatches — fog, key light and ground all move together here.

02 — assets

How the tesserae are laid

The four Roman laying patterns, in the mood you selected above. This is the most load-bearing asset in the game: it is the ground, the progress bar and the premise at once, and it is what ADR 8's detail mandate mostly means in practice.

03 — assets

The restorer

Articulated figures on a joint frame, with a labourer's proportions rather than a hero's — head about a seventh of the height, weight on the back foot, the working arm reaching down to the floor. All four wear an apron and a belt, carry a satchel with tesserae showing in it, and hold the tool their build is named for. These replace the placeholder octahedron.

04 — assets

The scavenger

Built as anatomy rather than debris: a plated carapace on jointed legs that lift out of the body and plant on the floor, a head with mandibles, and on some builds a grasping arm. Nothing on the body is lit — the only saturated thing a scavenger carries is what it has taken, so your eye goes to the theft.

05

The board as it stands

Generated with Three.js under the same true-isometric orthographic camera the game uses — direction (1, √2⁄2, 1), the angle that projects a cube to a regular hexagon. Tesserae are instanced chamfered extrusions, figures are articulated joint frames of tapered bones and rounded slabs; every placement, rotation, height and colour jitter comes from one seeded generator, exactly as packages/sim's PRNG feeds the renderer in the real client.
